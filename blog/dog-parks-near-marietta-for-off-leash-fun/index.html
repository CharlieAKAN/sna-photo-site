Dog Parks Near Marietta for Off-Leash Fun

A simple guide to two verified off-leash dog areas near Marietta, with rules that matter before you plan dog photography time.

October 5, 2026
Snout notes

Relaxed sessions begin before photo day. A little preparation helps your pet arrive ready to explore.

Plan your session

Dog parks near Marietta that are easy to use

When families ask us about places where a dog can stretch out before or after a photo session, we try to keep the answer simple and practical. We also want to stay accurate. So for this guide, we stuck to off-leash areas we could verify on official pages.

If you are planning dog photography in Marietta, an off-leash park is not always the right photo location. Most fenced dog areas are built for play, not portraits. Still, they can be very helpful for a quick decompression stop, a sniff break, or a bit of exercise before a calmer session somewhere else.

The City of Marietta lists two official off-leash areas in its Parks & Recreation Facilities pages: Lewis Park and Wildwood Park. The city also has a dedicated Lewis Park facility page with hours, amenities, and park rules. For dogs who stay leashed well and families who want a trail feel instead, the National Park Service confirms that pets are welcome at Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield Park, but they must stay on a handheld leash no longer than six feet.

1) Lewis Park in Marietta

Lewis Park is one of the easiest official options to point people toward because the city provides clear details. The park is at 475 Campbell Hill Street in Marietta, and the city lists hours of 5:00 AM to 11:00 PM. The facility page says the dog area is almost an acre, completely fenced, and includes a water spigot plus shade structures for people. The broader park also has parking, a walking track, benches, picnic tables, and restrooms, which makes it easier for a family visit or a quick stop before portraits.

For many dogs, that setup can be useful before a Marietta pet photography session. A short visit can take the edge off high energy. It can also give shy dogs a little time to settle into a new day before we ask them to focus around a camera.

The rules matter here. On the official Lewis Park page, the city says dogs must be leashed until inside the double-gated entry area, then removed from leash before entering the off-leash area. Owners must keep dogs in sight and under voice control, clean up after them, and keep leashes in hand. The city also says no food for humans or dogs, including treats, is allowed within the fenced area. That last point is worth remembering if your dog usually works for snacks during training.

If you are thinking about portraits, we would treat Lewis Park as a pre-session play stop rather than the main portrait location. Busy off-leash areas can be distracting, and many dogs find it hard to hold still there. We usually get calmer, cleaner images in a quieter nearby spot after the play break.

2) Wildwood Park in Marietta

The City of Marietta also lists Wildwood Park as an official off-leash area on its Parks & Recreation Facilities page. In the city park listings, Wildwood Park is described as a 28.11 acre park with a jogging trail, hiking trails, playground, and a dog off-leash area. That mix can be nice for families who want a little more room around the dog park part of the outing.

Because the city clearly identifies Wildwood as an off-leash area on its official pages, it belongs on a short list for local dog owners who want a verified option. If your goal is exercise first and photos second, a larger park footprint can help you build in time to walk, let your dog settle, then move to a simpler portrait setting.

As always, check the park page before you head out. Hours, amenities, and closures can change, and city facility pages are the best place to confirm the current setup.

What about Kennesaw Mountain?

Kennesaw Mountain is one of the best known outdoor spots near Marietta, but it is not an off-leash dog park. The National Park Service says pets are welcome on trails, though not in the Visitor Center, and they must be on a handheld leash no longer than six feet. The park also reminds visitors to bag and bin waste, respect wildlife, and bring plenty of water for dogs.

That makes Kennesaw Mountain a different kind of option. It can work well for leashed portraits with families who want a natural look and a sense of place. It is not the spot for fetch, free running, or loose-dog social time. If you want both, you might pair a quick off-leash stop at a city dog park with a separate portrait session in a quieter leashed location.

How we would choose between them

Choose Lewis Park if you want:

  • A clearly documented off-leash area with posted rules
  • Fenced space for a short energy break
  • Easy amenities like parking, restrooms, and a walking path

Choose Wildwood Park if you want:

  • An official Marietta off-leash area with more overall park space
  • A visit that includes walking trails or a broader family outing
  • A simple exercise stop before portraits elsewhere

Choose Kennesaw Mountain if you want:

  • Leashed outdoor portraits with a more natural backdrop
  • Trails and scenic atmosphere
  • A location where you can combine a walk with photos, while following NPS leash rules

A few practical notes before you go

Bring water, waste bags, and a regular leash. If your dog gets overstimulated in busy dog spaces, skip the off-leash park and choose a quieter walk instead. If your dog guards toys or gets too excited around groups, that is another good reason to keep the play portion simple and short.

For photos, we usually tell families not to wear their dog out completely. The sweet spot is a dog who has moved a little and relaxed, but still has enough spark to engage with you. That is where expression shows up.
