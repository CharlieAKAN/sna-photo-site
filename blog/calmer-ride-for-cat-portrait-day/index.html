A Calmer Ride for Cat Portrait Day

A simple plan for carrier practice, car rides, and a quiet arrival so your cat can feel safer on portrait day.

October 26, 2026
Snout notes

Relaxed sessions begin before photo day. A little preparation helps your pet arrive ready to explore.

Plan your session

Portrait day starts before the camera comes out

Most cats do not mind the photos nearly as much as the trip. The hard part is often the carrier, the car ride, and arriving in a new place. If we make those steps easier, the portrait session usually feels easier too.

That is why we like to think about cat portraits as a travel day, not just a photo day. The American Veterinary Medical Association says cats should ride in a carrier so they feel secure and cannot escape or get under the driver’s feet during the trip. The same AVMA travel guidance also recommends helping pets get used to the carrier ahead of time and adding a familiar blanket or toy for comfort. You can read that guidance here: AVMA traveling with your pet.

For cats, that small bit of planning can change the whole tone of the session.

Start with the carrier a few days early

If the carrier only appears right before something stressful, your cat learns to avoid it. The American Association of Feline Practitioners explains that the visit starts at home and that carrier size, carrier choice, and carrier training all matter for reducing distress. Their cat transport guidance also supports secure placement of the carrier in the vehicle. A helpful client handout is here: AAFP guide to getting your cat to the vet.

We suggest bringing the carrier out several days before your session if you can. Leave it open in a quiet room. Put a blanket inside that already smells like home. You can also add a favorite bed if it fits safely.

Let your cat investigate on their own. No forcing. No closing the door yet. The goal is simple: the carrier becomes part of the room, not a trap.

What helps most

  • Set the carrier out early, with the door open.
  • Place soft bedding inside.
  • Add a familiar scent from home.
  • Toss a few treats in and let your cat come and go.
  • Practice once or twice with the door closed for a very short time, then reward and release.

If your cat is already very worried about carriers, keep this light and brief. We want calm repetition, not a battle.

Practice the car ride, not just the carrier

Some cats step into the carrier just fine and then get upset when the car starts moving. The AVMA recommends giving pets time to get used to travel before a longer trip. For portrait day, that can mean one or two short practice rides around the block, followed by a quiet return home and something pleasant.

This matters because cats learn from patterns. If every ride ends in stress, the ride itself becomes the warning sign. A short, uneventful trip can help soften that pattern.

Keep the carrier level in the car and place it in a stable spot. Avoid blasting music. Drive smoothly. Talk less than you think you need to. Many cats do better when the whole ride feels boring.

Keep the day simple

On session day, less activity is usually better. Try not to squeeze the portrait session in between errands. The AVMA also advises never leaving pets unattended in a car, even for a short stop, so a direct trip is the safest plan.

At home, give yourself extra time. Cats notice when we rush. If you are calm, your cat often has a better chance of staying calm too.

We also recommend packing very lightly for cat sessions. Bring only what helps your cat settle.

A short cat session packing list

  • The carrier your cat already practiced with
  • A familiar blanket or small towel
  • A few favorite treats, if your cat enjoys them
  • A small brush, if your cat likes being brushed
  • Any comfort item your cat already uses at home

You do not need a big bag of supplies. For most cats, familiar is better than fancy.

Think in terms of comfort, not performance

We do not expect cats to "pose" the way dogs sometimes do. A good cat portrait session is usually built around small, honest moments: a stretch, a look over the shoulder, paws tucked under, bright eyes from a favorite perch or blanket.

That is why the best prep often looks very ordinary. A calm carrier. A safe ride. A little time to settle. Those things matter more than trying to train new behaviors right before the session.

The AAFP notes that preparing for transport and helping cats acclimate to the carrier can reduce stress before the visit even begins. That lines up closely with what we see in real portrait sessions. When the trip goes better, the pictures usually do too.

If your cat gets upset in the car

Some cats cry, pant, drool, or hide when they travel. If that sounds like your cat, keep your session plans gentle and talk with your veterinarian before portrait day. General travel advice from the AVMA says your veterinarian is the right person to ask if your pet struggles with travel or motion sickness. We cannot give medical advice, but we can plan a slower, simpler session on our side.

That might mean a shorter appointment, more breaks, or a setup that asks less from your cat right away. Our job as an Atlanta area pet photographer is not to push through stress. It is to notice it and work around it when we can.

What we watch for when your cat arrives

When a cat first comes in, we look for the small signs. Are they peeking out? Freezing? Leaning into the blanket? Curious? Those details help us choose the pace.

Sometimes the best first step is leaving your cat in the open carrier for a minute so they can look around. Sometimes it is letting them move onto a blanket that smells like home. Sometimes it is doing almost nothing for a little while.

That slower start is not wasted time. It is often the reason we get natural cat portraits instead of tense ones.

A good session can still be a quiet one

Not every cat will give us big movement or lots of variety, and that is okay. Quiet portraits can be beautiful. A steady gaze, a paw tucked over the edge of a blanket, the shape of whiskers against soft light, those are meaningful details too.

For cat families who want Marietta pet photography, the goal is not to turn your cat into a different kind of pet for one day. It is to make enough room for your cat to feel safe being themselves.

If you are planning a cat portrait session and want to talk through the travel piece first, we are always happy to help you think it through. When you are ready, we would love to welcome you and create portraits at a pace that feels kind to your cat.
