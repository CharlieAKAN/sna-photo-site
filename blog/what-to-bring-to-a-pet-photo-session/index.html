What to Bring to a Pet Photo Session

A simple packing list for a smoother pet photo session, with comfort, safety, and great expressions in mind.

July 27, 2026
Snout notes

Relaxed sessions begin before photo day. A little preparation helps your pet arrive ready to explore.

Plan your session

Getting ready for a pet photo session does not need to feel complicated. A short packing list can make the whole experience smoother for you, your pet, and your photographer. We have found that the best sessions usually start with a few familiar items, a little planning, and realistic expectations.

If you are wondering what to bring, think about two goals. First, help your pet feel safe and comfortable. Second, make it easy to reward good moments when they happen. The American Kennel Club notes that treasured toys and rewards can help dogs relax and have fun during photos, which is exactly what we want in a session. See AKC's dog photo tips for a helpful overview.

Start with the everyday basics

Bring the gear you already use in daily life. A well-fitted collar or harness, a regular leash, waste bags, and fresh water are the basics we never want to skip. For many pets, familiar gear lowers stress because it smells and feels normal.

If your pet rides to the session in a crate, make sure it is one they can stand, sit, lie down, and turn around in comfortably. The CDC also recommends securing a crate so it does not slide in the car, or using a harness attached to the seat buckle if your pet rides on the seat. Their pet travel guidance is also a good reminder to bring water, a bowl, medication if needed, and travel documents when appropriate. You can read that advice at CDC Pet Travel Safety.

  • Collar or harness that fits well
  • Standard leash
  • Waste bags
  • Water and a bowl
  • Carrier or crate if your pet uses one
  • Any needed medication your veterinarian has already prescribed

Pack more treats than you think you need

Small, easy-to-eat treats do a lot of work during a pet photo session. They help with attention, position changes, and happy expressions. AKC recommends bringing lots of bite-sized treats and even a mix of regular and high-value options so you can switch things up if your dog loses interest. That advice comes from their training guidance on what to bring to class, and it translates well to photo sessions too.

We like treats that are tiny, not crumbly, and quick to chew. The point is not to feed a full snack. The point is to reward fast, then move on before your pet loses focus. If your pet has food allergies or a sensitive stomach, bring only treats you know are safe for them.

  • Small soft treats
  • A few higher-value treats for harder moments
  • A treat pouch or easy-to-reach pocket
  • Your pet's usual food rewards if they have diet limits

Bring one or two favorite toys

A favorite toy can help us get alert ears, a curious head tilt, or a playful burst of energy. It can also give your pet something familiar to focus on in a new place. AKC's pet photo guidance suggests bringing treasured toys and using familiar games or favorite tricks to keep the experience fun.

Try not to bring a whole toy basket. Too many choices can get distracting. One squeaky toy, one tug toy, or one small cat teaser is usually enough. If your pet has a comfort item, that can help too, even if it stays just off camera between setups.

Do a quick grooming kit

You do not need a full spa bag, but a few cleanup items are worth it. Pets pick up slobber, dust, loose fur, and grass faster than most people expect. A brush, comb, towel, and pet-safe wipes can save the day between shots.

If your dog drools after drinking, a small towel helps keep the chest and muzzle neat. If your cat sheds when nervous, a quick brush can help settle the coat. Keep grooming light and familiar. Right before a session is not the time to try a new product or a big style change.

  • Brush or comb
  • Small towel
  • Pet-safe wipes
  • Lint roller for your clothes

Think about comfort, not just looks

If your pet already has a bandana, bow tie, sweater, or collar they wear comfortably, bring it along. If they hate wearing accessories, skip them. Good expressions matter more than forced styling.

For studio or indoor sessions, a small blanket or mat can help some pets settle between setups. AKC includes a blanket or towel on its class-prep list for dogs, and that same idea works well during portraits. Familiar fabric can give a nervous pet a clear place to rest while we reset lights, props, or backgrounds.

Do not forget safety for the ride

Getting to the session safely is part of session prep. The CDC says pets should not roam freely in the car, and they should never be left alone in a parked vehicle. The CDC's heat guidance adds that even with a cracked window, the temperature inside a car can rise dangerously fast. In warm Georgia weather, that is especially worth taking seriously.

If your session is outdoors, it also helps to pack with the weather in mind. Bring extra water and plan for shade and breaks. If your pet is sensitive to heat, tell your photographer before the session so the pace and location can be adjusted.

Watch your pet's stress signals

One of the most useful things you can bring is awareness. Fear Free Happy Homes points out that subtle body language changes are often the first sign that a pet is uncomfortable. Lip licking, yawning when not tired, turning away, pinned ears, or showing a lot of white around the eyes can all mean your pet needs a break.

That matters more than any prop. A calm, supported pet photographs better than a stressed pet in a perfect outfit. If your dog or cat is nervous in new places, let your photographer know ahead of time. We can often keep things simpler, slower, and quieter when we know what helps your pet feel safe.

A simple packing list to save on your phone

  • Collar or harness
  • Leash
  • Waste bags
  • Water and bowl
  • Favorite treats
  • One or two favorite toys
  • Brush or comb
  • Towel and pet-safe wipes
  • Blanket or mat
  • Any familiar accessory your pet actually likes wearing
  • Any veterinarian-prescribed medication your pet may need that day

The goal is not to bring everything you own. It is to bring the few things that help your pet feel like themselves. That is what leads to relaxed faces, bright eyes, and images that really feel true to your life together.
